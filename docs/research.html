Can a provenance mark
survive splitting and mixing?

Someone steals, moves, splits, mixes. A trail remains. Then what? SatoTag explores how an evidence-linked mark can propagate through a synthetic Bitcoin-style transaction graph, and who should be allowed to authorize it.

Try the interactive demoInspect the sourceBring a reproducible failure

Research prototype, synthetic data only. This is an accounting and governance experiment. It does not modify Bitcoin, certify a theft, impose a market price, validate real-chain deposits or connect to a wallet. “Mixing” here means merging synthetic inputs; no claim is made to trace arbitrary real-world CoinJoins or cross-asset routes.

Follow the exposure

Exact fractional accounting propagates modeled exposure through splits and merges. A tiny exposure remains visible without labeling the whole balance as stolen.

Question the authority

Ed25519 case reports, reviewer quorum, a linked decision history and corrections that preserve the record. Signatures identify keys; they do not establish the truth of an allegation.

Check the keys

The v0.2.1 authorization layer checks root-signed key lifetimes, current revocations and reserve replacement. Independently current registry and key-manifest checkpoints are required.

Run it. Change an input. Show the failure.

The v0.2.1 engine passed 85 project tests on Python 3.11, 3.12 and 3.13. This is a reproducible baseline, not an independent audit. The browser demo displays reports generated by Python; test the Python verifier to challenge authorization and signature handling.

Open questions worth testing

  • Can altered receipts, duplicate reviewers or revoked keys cross the documented verifier boundaries?
  • Can splits, merges or corrections break conservation of value or modeled exposure?
  • What happens when allegations arrive late, evidence is false, or reviewers collude?
  • How should independent checkpoints, key custody and recovery work in a real deployment?

Minimal synthetic reproductions and architectural criticism are both welcome. Include the commit, input, command and expected versus actual outcome in the public review thread.
